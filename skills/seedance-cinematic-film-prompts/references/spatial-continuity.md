# World Space, Screen Space, and Axis Continuity

Read this reference for interior/exterior coverage, doorways, counters, streets, corridors, pursuits, action geography, or scenes with several camera positions.

## Separate two coordinate systems

World space is the physical set and remains stable:

```text
The main door opens onto the street.
The counter stands toward the back, opposite the entrance.
Medicine cabinets stand behind the counter, with a working aisle between them.
Viewed from the entrance toward the cabinets, the passage is at frame right of the counter.
Character A is on the customer side; character B is in the working aisle.
```

Screen space belongs only to a specific camera:

```text
In this shot the doorway is frame right.
Character A occupies left foreground facing frame right.
Character B is right midground facing frame left.
```

Never promote one shot's screen-left or screen-right into a group-wide geographic rule when the camera changes.

## Scene-map template

Before drafting shots, define:

```text
Set orientation:
Fixed anchors:
Entrances and exits:
Character starting positions:
Character paths:
Primary interaction axis:
Preferred camera bank:
Available neutral or transition shots:
World-space light sources:
```

Default to physical anchors rather than compass directions. A left/right floor-plan statement must name its viewing direction. Clear relationships such as `inside/outside`, `street side/counter side`, `window side/wall side`, or `uphill/downhill` are sufficient when they are unambiguous.

## Dialogue axis

- Define the line connecting the principal speakers.
- Keep cameras on one bank of that line for ordinary shot/reverse-shot coverage.
- Preserve opposite eyelines even though characters may switch screen sides in doorway or near-axis angles.
- An over-the-shoulder view from inside toward outside and its reverse from outside toward inside are valid when real geography remains legible.
- Do not use `不越轴` to prohibit useful camera positions. Use it to prohibit an unexplained orientation flip.

## Crossing or rebuilding an axis

Cross only with an observable reorientation:

- a neutral frontal or directly-on-axis shot;
- a camera move visibly crossing behind or around a character;
- a character walking through a doorway or past the other character;
- a new establishing shot that clearly rebuilds positions.

Do not jump directly to the opposite bank while preserving identical blocking; this makes eyelines and travel direction appear reversed.

## Lighting continuity

Anchor lighting to the set: `daylight enters through the main door`; `the oil lamp is beside the stack of books on the counter`. Record whether the lamp is lit and its limited reach. Derive each shot’s screen direction and visible facial shading from those sources and the character’s orientation. A camera reversal alone does not swap the illuminated anatomical cheek. Preserve the latest approved footage’s light and exposure relationship unless the story or user changes it.

When a user specifies light from a person's left/right, distinguish anatomical direction from screen direction. Anchor the source to the person's initial stance and a fixed set location, then derive shading as they turn. Record source color/quality, direction, fill or bounce, and practical-light reach once at group level. Shot instructions describe only relevant changes: a head turn alters the lit area, a hat casts a shadow, or an object enters the light. Avoid resetting exposure, tinting skin blue to express cold light, or adding cloud flicker without a motivated weather change.

## Movement and action geography

- Fix the direction of hazards, escape routes, water, traffic, or pursuit in world space.
- State movement from one anchor to another.
- Screen direction may change after a motivated camera change, but the physical destination must not.
- For impacts or falls, show the cause, loss of balance, contact, and settling rather than teleporting between body states.

## Accessible routes and prop continuity

For a counter, desk, doorway, or other barrier, establish the accessible side before the interaction. Example: the character walks around the visible counter end into the aisle between counter and cabinets, then opens a drawer facing that aisle. Keep the decisive passage visible when an apparent teleport or reach through furniture would otherwise result.

A useful top-down diagram distinguishes fixed furniture, character starting/ending positions, character paths, camera paths, and the dialogue axis. Mark the establishing viewpoint so diagram left/right is not mistaken for every shot’s left/right.

Track who holds a prop before and after each handoff, including the holding hand, height, orientation, and any portion returned to the giver. On an action cut, describe the same movement resuming in the next framing. Keep received props present through later shots until a motivated return, storage, or release. For example, a hand takes a sample in close-up; the cut continues the withdrawing hand into a medium shot where the person examines it. Choose only the fragile contact details needed for continuity.

For a handoff, let the receiver secure the object before the giver releases it. Supporting actors may make room or lean in when the shared task motivates it; avoid adding several simultaneous intricate hand actions.

For a fall or loss, show the support or grip that fails and the force that moves the object. For example, a person clings to a rock to stop sliding, leaving only one arm to protect a basket; a stronger surge dislodges it, and the need to keep holding the rock prevents pursuit. Use this as causal reasoning, not as a required flood-scene formula.

For a requested continuous take, rehearse the route internally: can the camera and every visible character reach each position without crossing furniture? Keep following characters present until their departure from the route is motivated. Begin at the latest useful action, especially when the previous group already showed entry.
